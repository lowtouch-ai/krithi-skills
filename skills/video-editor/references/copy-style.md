# Copy style for generated text

Applies to chapter titles, and later to clip titles and post captions. Condensed from lowtouch.ai's writing rules: write like a person who knows the subject, not like a press release.

## Always
- **No em dashes.** Use a comma, a period, a semicolon or parentheses.
- **Say the real thing.** Specific nouns and numbers from the recording. Chapter titles name the topic as the speaker did ("Why teams review first", "Per-seat pricing"), not a summary of a summary.
- **Sentence case** for titles ("Pricing for small teams"), not Title Case.
- **Short.** A chapter title is two to six words.
- **Spell names as the company does:** `lowtouch.ai` (lower case, with the dot), `Krithi`.

## Never
- Hype and filler words: unlock, supercharge, elevate, empower, game-changer, revolutionary, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, "dive deep", "in today's fast-paced world".
- "Not just X, it's Y" and "not only X but also Y". The checker rejects the words **"not just"** and **"not only"** anywhere in a title or caption, so rephrase ("Fast reviews", not "Fast reviews, not just fast merges").
- Any of these, which the checker also rejects: unlock, supercharge, elevate, empower, game-changer, revolutionary, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, deep dive, delve, leverage, harness, "in today's", "excited to announce", "testament to", tapestry.
- Trailing "-ing" summaries ("...streamlining operations and driving efficiency").
- Padded triplets ("innovative, transformative, and groundbreaking").
- Openers like "Excited to announce" or "Great question".
- Words the speaker did not say, as if they were quotes. Never put invented words in quotation marks.

## Social post captions (when shorts exist)
Lead with the sharpest real line or number from the clip. Short lines. At most one or two hashtags, often none. No emoji unless it truly fits. End on a thought, not a call to action cliché.

## Check before showing the user
Read each title aloud. If it sounds like a template, rewrite it from what was actually said.
